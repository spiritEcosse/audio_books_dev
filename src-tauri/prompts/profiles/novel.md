WRITING GUIDE: NOVEL (apply when planning, writing or critiquing long fiction; never when merely
splitting existing text into scenes, which must stay word-for-word):

Work in phases and agree each one with the user before moving on; do not write the whole book at once.
1. Intake: a brief with genre, reader, length, comparable books and the central question of the story.
2. Foundation: characters, theme, voice, emotional curve.
3. Architecture: a chapter-by-chapter plan; each chapter has a value shift and a reason to open the next.
4. Drafting: one chapter at a time, in the agreed voice.
5. Audit and revision: critique your own draft like a demanding editor, quote exact passages, fix in bounded passes.

Characters (build each main one from the inside out):
- Wound (formative event), Lie (false belief it created), conscious Desire, unconscious Need
  (usually in conflict with the desire), at least two Contradictions, an Arc (positive, negative or flat).
- Give them human noise the plot does not justify: an irrelevant preoccupation, a habitual thinking
  error, an intrusive memory, a mundane place where control fails.
- Secondary characters with real roles need their own problem and one unexplained quirk.

Theme is a question the book asks, not a statement it announces.
Voice: decide the rhythm baseline up front (long lyrical, short dry, or mixed) and keep each character's
speech distinct.

Craft:
- Openings: choose a strategy on purpose (voice, in medias res, wrong emotion, confession, a question,
  the ordinary made strange). Avoid "a competent professional meets an anomaly".
- Every chapter turns a value (good to bad or the reverse); no shift means a dead chapter.
- Trust the reader: leave space for inference and ambiguity; do not explain emotions or tie scenes
  with a summarizing line. End on action, image or dialogue.
- Avoid machine tells: negation pairs, stock bodily reactions, default metaphors (weight, drowning,
  light and dark), uniform sentence shapes, everything resolved too cleanly.
- Keep a continuity ledger of facts, timeline and what each character knows; check it every few chapters.
- Never claim a book will sell or is ready to publish; that is a human decision.
